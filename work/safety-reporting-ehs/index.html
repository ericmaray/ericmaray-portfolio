01 — THE CHALLENGEAccess is part
of safety.

A reporting system only works when people use it.

Safety reporting had been distributed across multiple forms and processes. That made an already-sensitive task feel more administrative than helpful, particularly when people needed to report a hazard, near miss, injury, incident, or property damage quickly.

I approached the problem as more than a form redesign: how could one familiar entry point make reporting accessible across the organization, improve the quality of information captured, and support more timely follow-through?

02 — THE EXPERIENCEOne entry point.
Relevant questions.

THE DROP: a simpler way to speak up.

The Safety Reporting Form — promoted through the internal THE DROP experience — was designed to capture different kinds of events without making every employee navigate every possible question.

Accessible entry

Direct link and QR-code access to the form, with the intranet offering a familiar starting point for employees.

Less repetitive typing

An employee-ID lookup designed to populate known identifying information, with manual entry when a match was unavailable.

Adaptive questions

Event type, date, time, and location guide follow-up questions so reports collect the details relevant to the situation.

Useful evidence

Event information and supporting details create a stronger basis for supervisor review, safety follow-up, and trend analysis.

The design principle: reduce the effort required to report a concern without reducing the quality of information needed to act on it.
03 — THE WORKFLOWA report is
the beginning.

Designing for what happens next.

The form was designed as an easier point of entry, but the operating process had to begin earlier and continue further. A supervisor-focused reporting and response guide developed with the Risk & Benefits Analyst emphasized immediate care, prompt notification, the minimum information needed to act, and continued support through recovery. Reporting should enable that response—not delay it.

05 — THE TAKEAWAYBetter systems
shape behavior.

From collecting reports to creating visibility.

The completed reporting work provided a more accessible route for raising concerns, with a consolidated design and more intentional data capture. The wider EHS platform advanced as a developing system rather than a fully completed rollout.

The financial case also mattered: replacing the legacy FastField approach was estimated to offer substantial annual savings, but that figure was a projection, not verified realized savings. I chose not to present it as an achieved financial outcome.

What the project demonstrates: experience design, low-friction employee access, conditional form logic, workflow thinking, data structure, and the ability to connect safety culture with practical digital systems.

A form can capture an event. A well-designed system can make it easier for people to speak up, help others respond, and reveal what the organization needs to learn.
